# Stock Trading Strategy Backtest Project

* <h4><b> Overview </b></h4> 

  The analysis explores the correlation between ANZ, BHP, CBA, RIO, and WES stock prices, revealing strong positive relationships, particularly between ANZ and CBA. A moving average strategy using 5-period and 20-period averages highlights key buy and sell signals, demonstrating its potential effectiveness. The heatmap visualization confirms clustering in stock movements, emphasizing the importance of diversification. Overall, the study underscores how technical indicators can aid investment decisions by identifying trends and optimizing trade timing.

* <h4><b> Key Findings </b></h4> 

  - <b> Strong Stock Correlations – </b> ANZ and CBA show the highest positive correlation, suggesting similar price movements, while other stocks also exhibit strong relationships.
  - <b> Effective Moving Average Strategy – </b> The 5-period and 20-period moving averages successfully generate buy and sell signals, indicating potential profitability in trend-following strategies.
  - <b> Volatility Patterns – </b> Stocks like BHP and RIO exhibit more fluctuations, impacting trade timing and requiring careful risk management.
  - <b> Clustering in Stock Movements – </b> The heatmap visualization confirms that stocks move in groups, highlighting sector-based trends and the need for diversification.
  - <b> Backtesting Insights – </b> Historical performance suggests that this strategy captures market trends but may require fine-tuning for different market conditions.

* <h4><b> Insightful Details </b></h4> 

  - <b> Consistent Mean Reversion – </b> Stocks exhibit periods of mean reversion, where prices tend to return to historical averages, influencing trade timing.
  - <b> Moving Average Lag Effect – </b> The strategy's reliance on moving averages introduces a lag, meaning signals may occur after a significant price movement has already happened.
  - <b> Sector Influence on Correlation – </b> The strong correlation between ANZ and CBA suggests that banking stocks move together, while mining stocks (BHP, RIO) also show synchronized trends.
  - <b> Trend Confirmation Through Clustering – </b> The clustering heatmap validates that stocks exhibit grouped behavior, reinforcing sector-based investment strategies.
  - <b> Potential Overtrading Risk – </b> The strategy may trigger frequent trades in highly volatile stocks, increasing transaction costs and reducing net profitability.

* <h4><b> Challenges </b></h4> 
 
  - <b> Lag in Moving Average Signals – </b> The strategy reacts to price changes with a delay, potentially missing optimal entry and exit points.
  - <b> High False Signals in Volatile Markets – </b> Frequent short-term fluctuations can trigger unnecessary buy/sell signals, leading to overtrading.
  - <b> Transaction Costs Impact Profitability – </b> Frequent trading increases costs, which can significantly reduce overall returns, especially in high-frequency strategies.
  - <b> Correlation Limits Diversification – </b> Strong correlation among stocks within the same sector means the strategy may not provide sufficient diversification benefits.
  - <b> Historical Bias in Backtesting – </b> Past performance does not guarantee future results, and the strategy may not adapt well to different market conditions.
